# [100 CRAFTING / SCRATCHBUILDING / KITBASHING Tips For Beginners!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E8odG8dC3go)
## by **Bill Making Stuff**

### 1. Use Junk
Start with plastic junk from recycling bins, old cardboard, and paper. You don't need expensive materials to be creative and make what you want.

### 2. Take Things Apart
Disassemble spray bottles, felt tip pens, and water pistols to get lots of little plastic shapes and components for building.

### 3. Cut Things Up
Don't be limited by the original shape - cut plastic bits and junk into the pieces you need. Make things smaller or use just sections.

### 4. Lids Make Good Wheels
Simple bottle caps and lids work perfectly as wheels when adorned with other details.

### 5. Use Beads
Beads are excellent crafting accessories with holes for armature wire. They can be shaped, melted, and stuck to things easily.

### 6. Card is Essential
Cardboard is cheap, abundant, and perfect for main structures. Cut it into squares and store it properly instead of recycling.

### 7. Super Glue + Baking Soda
Use baking soda sprinkled over super glue to create instant bonds and fill gaps in plastic.

### 8. Get the Silhouette Right
Focus on getting a good general shape first - it's easy to add details to good silhouettes, but bad silhouettes remain bad builds.

### 9. Use Armature Wire
Thread wire through builds to keep everything together and allow positioning before gluing in place.

### 10. Mix Materials
Combine any materials together - expanding foam with plastic, twigs with other elements. Paint unifies everything anyway.

### 11. Get Inspired
Take inspiration from books, films, TV shows, and games. Research makes building easier and more enjoyable.

### 12. Combine Glues
Use PVA glue for long-term holds and hot glue gun for quick bonds in the middle for permanent connections.

### 13. Pin It
Add structural support with pieces of wood, paper clips, or pins when building with foam and cardboard.

### 14. Sand Your Plastics
Create texture for glue to grip by sanding plastic surfaces before bonding.

### 15. Use Naked Flame
Carefully burn plastic surfaces to remove shine and make them more "stickable."

### 16. Make Your Own Materials
Create texture paste with glue, paint, baking powder, and sand instead of buying expensive materials.

### 17. Use Toys
Toys are model kits waiting to be disassembled - find engines, parts, and components for your builds.

### 18. Collaborate
Work with friends or others to stay motivated and inspired in your crafting.

### 19. Choose the Right Clay
Use air-drying clay for non-oven builds, Super Sculpey for detailed oven-safe work, or two-part epoxy clays for fine details.

### 20. Alternatives to Plastic Card
Use old plastic bottles, milk containers, or butter tubs instead of expensive plastic card sheets.

### 21. Tin Foil Armatures
Use aluminum foil for big, chunky armatures that can hold sculpting materials and help with curing.

### 22. Mix Stuff Into Stuff
Experiment by mixing offcuts, scraps, paint, and glue to create unique materials like "rusty ragu."

### 23. Hair Curlers
These make excellent grilles, fences, and can be heated and flattened into grid shapes.

### 24. Destroy Old Builds
Don't be afraid to take apart old builds to reuse good components in new projects.

### 25. Get a Rotary Tool
Overcome the fear of power tools - rotary tools make cutting, sanding, and drilling much easier.

### 26. Collect Hole Punches
Various hole punches create uniform shapes and rivets from plastic materials.

### 27. Wire Brushes and Rasps
Use these tools for quickly texturing plastic and wood, especially for wooden grain effects.

### 28. Keep a Sketchbook
Always have a sketchbook to capture ideas, make notes, and generate new concepts through doodling.

### 29. Toy Guns and Water Pistols
These provide ready-made sci-fi terrain pieces and useful internal components when disassembled.

### 30. Toilet Paper Mache
Mix torn toilet paper with water and PVA glue for a rough but effective paper mache alternative.

### 31. Twist Wire Armatures
Use a drill to twist multiple thin wires together to create strong, thick armature wire.

### 32. Oil Washes
Mix oil paints with mineral spirits to create washes that settle in recesses and highlight raised areas.

### 33. Use Greebles
Add small details like beads, sequins, and kit parts to make larger builds look more detailed and complex.

### 34. Forage for Materials
Search pound shops and go outside to find natural materials like twigs, wood chips, and acorns.

### 35. Bend with Heat
Remember that most plastics can be shaped and bent when heated with candles or heat guns.

### 36. EVA Foam Sheets
This versatile material can be cut, shaped, textured, and painted to look like various surfaces.

### 37. Homemade Weathering Pigments
Crush soft pastels in a pestle and mortar (or with a hammer) to create colored weathering powders.

### 38. Dinosaurs are Monsters
Start monster sculpts with dinosaur toys as a base - they already have claws and monster-like features.

### 39. Bulk Up with Cheap Stuff
Use polystyrene and cardboard to add volume to areas that won't be visible in the final build.

### 40. Kit Bash Your Own Minis
Create custom miniatures by combining parts - you'll feel more attached to your unique creations.

### 41. Check Stationary Sections
Find useful crafting materials like paper clips, pens, and drawing pins in the stationary aisles of any shop.

### 42. XPS Foam
Extruded polystyrene foam can be carved, detailed, and burned - excellent for bricks and architectural details.

### 43. Make Crusted
Create strong texture paste by mixing PVA glue, sand, and baking powder with water.

### 44. Visit Pet Shops and Garden Centers
Find clean flocking materials like fish tank sand, gravel, orchid bark, and aquarium plants.

### 45. Sandstone Technique
Stack XPS foam, carve horizontal lines, pick at the surface, and heat with a heat gun for realistic stone.

### 46. Mix Green Stuff and Milliput
Combine equal parts of both clays to get the best properties of each while making supplies last longer.

### 47. Details Make It
Add small details that show life and history - make terrain look lived-in and realistic.

### 48. Cardboard Pulp
Blend cardboard with water, strain, and mix with PVA glue to create a strong, recyclable material.

### 49. Build in Layers
Create complex builds by layering wires, cables, and other elements, then covering completely.

### 50. Make Your Own Kicker
Mix bicarbonate soda with water as a safer alternative to commercial CA glue accelerator sprays.

### 51. Use Conduit Wires
Plastic conduit casing creates excellent sci-fi organic metal muscle effects for terrain and monsters.

### 52. Rivets Make Everything Better
Add 1mm rivets to joints and metal plates to achieve convincing 28mm scale appearance.

### 53. Kit Bash with Anything
Don't limit yourself to official kit parts - use sequins, beads, screws, and other small objects creatively.

### 54. Wooden Stick Alternatives
Use candy floss sticks, coffee stirrers, and lollipop sticks instead of expensive balsa wood.

### 55. Twisty Trees
Mix different gauge wires, strings, and shoe laces together for more organic, natural-looking tree armatures.

### 56. Grid Method for Ideas
Draw a grid and refine ideas across multiple squares to develop and improve your designs.

### 57. Easy Tank Tracks
Cut EVA foam strips and add tread details on top for flexible, convincing tank tracks.

### 58. Wet Wipes as Canvas
Dry used wet wipes and cover with PVA glue to create scale model canvas for crates and coverings.

### 59. Alternative Bases
Use wooden discs from craft shops, charity shop coasters, or poker chips instead of expensive model bases.

### 60. Make Your Own Flock
Chop up jute twine finely and add small amounts of paint, then dry to create custom colored grass flock.

### 61. Head for Butt
When kit-bashing monsters, try switching the head and tail of toys to create instant alien creatures.

### 62. Texture EVA Foam
Use various tools like combs, leather working tools, and texture rollers to add surface details to foam.

### 63. Add Narrative
Give your builds a story or history to inform building decisions and make them more interesting.

### 64. Homemade Tufts
Cut bristles from old paint brushes or use coconut fiber to create grass tufts instead of buying them.

### 65. Hot Glue Crystals
Carve glue sticks into crystal shapes, dip in hot water, and color with transparent paint for gems.

### 66. Use Building Blocks
Standard building blocks and Jenga pieces make excellent bases for terrain that can be disguised later.

### 67. EVA Foam as Glue Helper
Use EVA foam between plastics that won't stick together - it bonds to everything with super glue.

### 68. Save Your Offcuts
Keep all cut-off pieces in a box for making junk piles and detailed bases later.

### 69. Acrylic Paint Pens
Keep paint pens handy for quick touch-ups, hazard stripes, and detail work without mixing paints.

### 70. Play Games
Actually use your terrain in games to inspire new builds and identify what pieces you need.

### 71. Paper Mache Clay
Mix tissue paper pulp with PVA glue, white plaster, flour, and baby oil for a clay-like material.

### 72. Corks for Rocks
Use wine corks, cork place mats, or beach-found corks to create realistic rock textures.

### 73. Don't Paint to the Edge
Leave metal or rust undercoat showing at edges to simulate wear and age.

### 74. Make Your Own Tools
Create specialized tools for specific problems - rivet makers, bead cutters, texture tools.

### 75. Guitar Strings for Wires
Use discarded guitar strings from music shops for scale cables and wires on 28mm models.

### 76. EVA Armor Plates
Thin EVA foam contours perfectly to create armor plating on miniatures.

### 77. Cake Package Textures
Look at the textured bottoms of cake packaging for interesting sci-fi surface patterns.

### 78. Use Cabochons
Hemispherical glass beads create convincing glowing monster and robot eyes.

### 79. Attend Conventions
Visit conventions to meet people, see new products, and get inspired by others' work.

### 80. Create Worlds
Build everything within a connected story world to inspire future builds and maintain narrative consistency.

### 81. Beach Combing
If you live near a beach, collect shells, driftwood, corks, and sand for crafting materials.

### 82. Coral Reef Technique
Dip coconut fibers in baking powder, cover in super glue, repeat for realistic coral formations.

### 83. Shop at Charity Shops
Find 90% of crafting materials at thrift stores - toys, tools, containers, and unusual items.

### 84. Try Other Creative Outlets
If you can't make something, try drawing or painting it instead to get the creative idea out.

### 85. Think Outside the Box
Buy weird items like false teeth, fake nails, and eyelashes to create unusual builds like gore mountains.

### 86. Try to Organize
Organize every small part, bead, and component into sections for easier access and more pleasant crafting.

### 87. Limitation Breeds Creativity
Challenge yourself by limiting materials to force creative problem-solving and improve skills.

### 88. Zenithal Prime
Spray black primer all over, then light gray/white from above only to create natural highlights and shadows.

### 89. Make Molds
Learn basic mold-making with silicone kits to duplicate useful components you've created.

### 90. Barnacle Technique
Use star-shaped rhinestones and glass beads with texture paste to create realistic barnacle clusters.

### 91. Oyumaru Molding
Use this Japanese remoldable putty in hot water for temporary, reusable molds.

### 92. UV Resin
Use UV-curing resin for quick small casts and transparent items like lenses and windows.

### 93. Print Decals
Print hazard stripes and signs on regular paper with inkjet printer and apply with Mod Podge.

### 94. Insect Wings
Score clear plastic packaging and scrunch it up to create realistic insect wing textures.

### 95. Fungus from Pom Poms
Soak mini pom poms in diluted Mod Podge and paint mixture for convincing mold and fungus effects.

### 96. Wooden Beads are Best
Wooden beads can be threaded for armatures and carefully carved/shaped for custom forms.

### 97. Foam Clay
This air-drying, flexible clay works in molds and creates EVA-foam-like results when cured.

### 98. Animal Hide Technique
Roll textured foam clay into flexible sheets for realistic animal skin and leather coverings.

### 99. Pyrography Tool
Use wood-burning tools to add battle damage, weather effects, and melt plastic for realistic damage.

### 100. Watch Videos
Learn from YouTube crafting channels - most tips come from watching others and trying techniques yourself.
